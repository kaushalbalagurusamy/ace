Lecture guidance

Matt Hulme, “Game Assets”, Gauntlet, 2 November 2026. Slide numbers are from the saved copy in docs/source/lecture-2026-11-02-game-assets.md. Each card says what the slide teaches, then what it means for a space racer built under this brief.

The lecture and the brief disagree.

The brief says All assets must be generated… Do not use pre-made assets. The lecture presents asset libraries (Kenney, Poly Haven, Freesound, Mixamo) as one of three routes (slides 26, 34, 53) and shows a racing demo, Sky Roadster, credited Library models + code-built scenery (slide 70). Read plainly, the library route is outside what the brief allows. This is finding F-052 and is an open question for the organisers, so this page treats routes 1 and 2 as the working set.

Choose the look before you generate anything

Slides 18, 25

Pick one art direction first (pixel, outlined vector, hand-painted, low-poly, stylised 3D, realistic). A coherent set shares palette, edges, scale and level of detail; individually good images still clash.

For us: the brief makes cohesion a pass/fail bar (mismatched styles… will fail the assignment). Write the style guide, with exact style wording as the lecture does on slides 19–24, before the first asset is made, and reuse it in every prompt.

Build the core interaction first, with plain shapes

Slides 13–15

Prototype the thing the game depends on using simple collision shapes; a small playable slice gives each new asset a place to be tested. Art direction and small asset tests can run alongside.

For us: the thing the game depends on is steering a ship under gravity so that it feels good and a track is winnable. Grey-box that first. Two players in one room finishing a race with circles for ships is the smallest slice worth testing.

Three routes to assets

Slides 26, 50

1 AI generation, 2 made with code, 3 asset libraries. The same split applies to audio.

For us: routes 1 and 2 only, mixed deliberately. Code does what code does well here (starfields, thrust and orbit trails, HUD, track overlays). Generation does hero art and music.

Create, critique with a separate agent, check in game, revise

Slides 10, 28

Generate from a reference and a small brief. A separate agent inspects the exported frames. Test scale, timing and readability in the build. Fix the biggest issue and review again.

For us: keep it, with two cautions the sweep raised. A critic from the same model family may share the generator's blind spots (F-067), and it has not been checked against human ratings (F-068). Cap the loop (F-069) and keep a provenance row for each asset.

The nearest demos to this game

Slides 69–73

Aftershock (space combat) credits code-drawn pixel art, code-built particles, Web Audio synthesis for SFX and generated music. Jelly Drop credits AI-generated scene art, code-rendered jelly, Web Audio SFX, generated music. On their credit lines both stay inside the brief's rule. Roofline and Dewbound list recorded sound; Sky Roadster lists library models.

For us: Aftershock is the closest template for look, effects and sound. The credit lines do not say whether “recorded” audio was self-recorded or premade, so do not copy that part without asking.

Made-with-code effects and geometry

Slides 31–33, 12, 62

Code can build an asset once and save it, or build it at start-up. Sprite Fusion publishes pixel-effect code (MIT TypeScript source, slide 33). Phaser is the 2D browser engine, Three.js the 3D one, each with a Vite starter.

For us: an orbital racer suits code: bodies, rings, trails, shields and boosts are shapes and particles. The engine choice is still an open decision (2D Phaser or 3D Three.js) for an ADR, not settled here.

Audio: generate or synthesise, then listen at real volume

Slides 50–54, 59

ElevenLabs makes sound effects and music from a prompt; jsfxr, Bfxr and Web Audio make retro cues in code. Play every sound in the game at its real volume before you keep it. Keep a text equivalent for spoken information.

For us: in a race, position and hazards cannot live in audio alone or colour alone (F-005). Give every cue a second channel.

UI, backgrounds and light

Slides 40, 41, 43

HUD, menus and type are assets too; keep key state visible and the selected thing obvious. Backgrounds can be layered images (2D) or a skybox (3D, slide 40; Skybox AI is listed on slide 36). Moving a light changes what players notice.

For us: the race HUD (position, lap, speed, boost) is the most-looked-at asset. Design it with the same care as the ships.

2D or 3D

Slides 6, 11

2D gives direct control of the final image but needs more frames per view and action (QuizCaster drew five views per character). 3D reuses one model across angles but needs materials, lighting and rigs.

For us (inference, not in the lecture): a top-down 2D view lets code rotate one ship sprite, so it avoids the multi-view frame cost. Needs a quick check on how a rotated pixel sprite looks before committing.

Agent skills and editor tools

Slides 60–64

A 77-skill game-dev collection, engine starters, and editor connections (Godot, Unity, Blender, Aseprite MCPs). Editors also have built-in automation: Blender Python and batch mode, Aseprite CLI plus Lua.

For us: prefer scripts over live connections. The Blender MCP executes arbitrary Python over an unauthenticated local socket and can import premade models (see Research seeds).

What the lecture does not cover

Multiplayer networking, physics simulation, hosting, the terms of AI generators, and how to disclose AI content to players. No slide addresses them. For a multiplayer space racer those are the places where the sweep found the most risk, so they are covered in the Minesweeping and Research seeds tabs and not in the course material.

  4. 4. Orbits: planets on analytic rails, only ships integrated

    Measured

    Two-body Kepler motion has a closed-form solution, so every client can compute where a planet is at any time t without stepping, with no drift and the same answer (primary reading, arXiv and Yoshida 1990, agent-read). Only the ships need a numerical integrator. In a local test (Node 24, one scenario, velocity-Verlet, dt 0.001, three gravity sources), summing the three pulls in a different order moved the ship by 0 at 1,000 steps, 1.7×10⁻¹³ at 10,000 and 9.7×10⁻¹⁰ at 100,000; a 1×10⁻¹² nudge to the start grew to 5.9×10⁻⁸ at 100,000. The orchestrator re-ran the script and got identical numbers.

    Reading it: over a race-length run the gap is tiny, and with two gravity sources it was exactly zero, so the hazard is real but small unless ships pass close to a body. The skeptic pass narrowed the claim: JavaScript fixes the order in which a given piece of code adds numbers, so identical code on every client sums identically; divergence needs different code paths (a server rewrite, a different iteration order), and the fixed body-index order is the guard. It also downgraded “clients will disagree” to plausible, because no second engine was run. Limits: one JavaScript engine only; the test ship was flung to about (−69, 28) by the last step; the ECMAScript spec only requires + − × ÷ and sqrt to match across engines, while Math.sin, cos, pow and the like may differ in the last bits. Use a fixed summation order, a fixed time step, and test Chrome, Safari and Firefox before trusting client-side prediction.

  5. 5. Netcode and hosting that survive Friday morning

    Verified live

    The research README's host claims were checked against vendor pages, and a separate skeptic agent re-fetched the Render, Vercel Hobby and PartyKit pages and confirmed them (the Cloudflare, Railway and npm figures were not re-checked). Render's free web service sleeps after 15 minutes without inbound traffic and takes about a minute to wake, which loses in-memory rooms; open WebSockets count as traffic. Vercel Hobby does support WebSockets today (the README said it could not), but connections close at 300 seconds and the plan is non-commercial. Hosted PartyKit shut down on 23 October 2026; PartyServer on your own Cloudflare account is the successor. Libraries are current and MIT: Colyseus 0.18.10 (published 2026-10-22), ws 8.22.1, from the npm registry. Cloudflare's free tier is hard-capped at 100,000 requests a day, so a limit shows up as an outage, not a bill.

    To settle: real cold-start time and how many rooms one small instance holds. Nobody measured either, and Railway's WebSocket page returned 404. Experiment: deploy a stub, race eight simulated players, wake it from idle.

    Hypothesis

The same bot becomes a practice opponent, which helps the “easy to get into a match” line when only one person is online.

  8. 8. Script Blender, do not connect to it

    Verified live

    The Blender MCP (MIT, 33.3k stars, actively maintained) exposes arbitrary Python execution on a local socket (default port 9876, localhost) with no authentication, and can import third-party models from Poly Haven and Sketchfab, which are premade assets. Stars are popularity, not vetting. If 3D is chosen, use Blender's command-line batch mode with scripts we wrote and reviewed (lecture slide 64), and keep imports out.
