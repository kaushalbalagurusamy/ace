The bar for every game
Your game should feel like a complete experience—not a tech demo or a single level.

Required design quality: Your game must have polished, high-quality graphics
and a cohesive visual style. Games with graphics that look thrown together, use
mismatched styles, or are generally poor quality will fail the assignment.
Make it substantial: Your game should have enough original, meaningful
gameplay to feel like a complete experience—not a single mechanic, a simple
clone, or a short demo stretched across multiple screens. Players should
encounter varied challenges and a clear sense of progression. Repetition or
cosmetic changes alone do not count as depth.
A clear objective: Players know what they are trying to accomplish and how to
play.

A beginning, middle, and end: Include an introduction, meaningful progression,

More than one moment of gameplay: Players encounter new challenges,

A unique, clean design: Your game should be visually distinctive, polished, and
cohesive. Its look, sound, and feel should reinforce its world and make the
experience meaningful.
Playable by someone else: A new player can get started without you standing
beside them. Include brief instructions where needed.
Browser access: Make your game playable in a web browser and provide a link
others can use. Players should not need to install the game.
Finish the details: Pay attention to layout, typography, animation, sound levels,
and feedback. Avoid a collection of default or mismatched assets; the result
should feel like one intentionally designed game.
GENERATE ALL
ASSETS
All assets must be generated for your game, including visuals and
audio such as sprites, characters, environments, sound effects, and
music. Do not use pre-made assets. Choose assets appropriate to
your genre and make them feel like they belong together.

Option 1: Exploration / Adventure
Inspiration: Ecco the Dolphin, The Legend of Zelda
Create a world that rewards players for exploring and gives them a reason to keep
going.
Your game should include
•
A clear central goal and a beginning-to-end story or journey.
•
At least three distinct, connected areas that players can explore and revisit.
•
At least three meaningful obstacles or puzzles that require more than simply
finding the exit.
•
A sense of progression: An ability, item, discovery, or change in the world opens
a new path.
•
A world with a consistent visual identity and an ending that resolves the central
goal.
•
Aim for a complete game that takes a new player roughly 10–15 minutes to
finish, with genuinely distinct challenges along the way. This is a target, not a
stopwatch-based pass/fail rule.
What makes it great: The world feels like a place, not a collection of rooms.
Exploration and discovery matter to the experience.
Option 2: Platformer
Inspiration: Super Mario Bros., Celeste
Create a game where movement is satisfying and players develop mastery as they
progress.
Your game should include
•
Responsive movement and a core mechanic that remains fun throughout the
game.
•
At least three distinct stages or sections, connected by a clear progression.
•
Increasing challenge: Introduce an obstacle or mechanic, then build on it.

Option 3: Multiplayer Racing
Inspiration: Mario Kart
Create a short, exciting racing game that people can play together and want to replay.
Your game should include
•
At least two people racing together, with a clear way to join the same game.
•
At least two distinct tracks or courses with different layouts or challenges.
•
A complete race flow: start, race, finish, results, and a way to play again.
•
Clear feedback on position or progress and who won.
•
At least one mechanic beyond basic steering and acceleration, such as boosts,
hazards, drifting, or power-ups.
•
Multiplayer that works reliably enough for players to complete a race together.
Individual races can be short, but the game should offer enough variety and multiplayer
polish to make people want to race again.
What makes it great: The racing feels good, it is easy to get into a match, and the
experience is fun over repeated short sessions.
